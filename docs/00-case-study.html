Case Study: Northwind Global

The fictional customer used in the delivery labs

Case Study Reference

Northwind Global is the fictional customer the delivery decision labs reason about. When a lab asks you to judge whether the customer is ready to start, or how to map their data into the OWP templates, this page is your source of truth. Keep it open alongside the labs.

💡 Keep This Page Open

The company profile, hierarchies, and data situation here are the reference for the two delivery labs — Data Readiness and Map the HR Extract. Whenever a lab asks you to make a call based on the customer's situation, come back here.

Company profile

About Northwind Global

  • Type: Global B2B technology & services company
  • Size: ~8,000 employees
  • Regions: North America (HQ Boston), EMEA (London), APAC (Singapore)
  • HCM system: Workday
  • Recruiting (ATS): Greenhouse
  • Headcount budget today: Excel, set annually by Finance

The pain — why OWP

Finance sets a headcount budget in a spreadsheet; HR creates positions in Workday ad hoc; Talent recruits from a separate req list. The three never reconcile — so Northwind over-hires in some orgs, under-hires in others, and Finance gets budget surprises every quarter.

There is no link between what's budgeted, what's planned, and what's being recruited. That's the closed-loop / plan-to-hire gap OWP closes.

What Northwind wants (scope)

  • Position-based planning with the WFB → CPP loop.
  • Selective access by region + function (an EMEA HRBP sees only EMEA).
  • A skills view for internal mobility — fill open roles internally first.
  • On worker cards: expose gender and performance rating, but not race / ethnicity (a per-customer DEI toggle — a good teaching point).
  • No role-grade planning — Northwind plans by role, not by grade. Don't enable Role Grade when it isn't needed.
💡 Teaching Points

Two of Northwind's asks are really "don't over-configure" lessons: the DEI toggle (expose gender and performance, but keep race / ethnicity off) and the Role Grade decision (leave it off — Northwind plans by role, not grade). Enabling either when the customer doesn't need it adds complexity for no benefit.

Organization & hierarchies

Northwind organizes work into three function / job families. The job codes below are the ones you'll see in the HR extract during the mapping lab.

FunctionRoles (job code)
GTM Account Executive (SLS-AE), Senior AE (SLS-AE-SR), Solutions Engineer (ENG-SE), Customer Success Manager (CS-CSM), Regional Sales Director (SLS-MGR)
Engineering Software Engineer (ENG-SW), Senior Software Engineer (ENG-SW-SR), Pre-Sales Engineer (ENG-SE), Engineering Manager (ENG-MGR)
G&A Financial Analyst (FIN-ANL), HR Business Partner (HR-HRBP), Counsel (LGL-CNS), General Counsel (LGL-GC)

Management (supervisory) org

Exists in Workday. Example orgs: Global Sales → EMEA Enterprise Sales, NA Customer Success, APAC Engineering, NA Finance, Legal.

This drives half the app — the supervisory-org rollup is mandatory.

Budget hierarchy (5 levels)

Total Company → Function → Region → Department → Cost Center.

Not yet structured in a tool — it lives in the Finance spreadsheet. Needed for WFB; you can start with a dummy 5-level hierarchy and refine.

📝 Job Levels / Job Catalog

Job levels and the job catalog are maintained in Workday. They are foundational: bring them into OWP, but do not create new job levels in OWP.

📝 How this case study is used

Northwind Global is invented teaching material — it gives the delivery methodology something concrete to reason about. The delivery labs (Data Readiness Assessment, Map the HR Extract) reason about Northwind's situation and data. The application labs (in WFB, CPP, and Security) use the training tenant's own Golden Copy data instead, so they don't reference Northwind.
