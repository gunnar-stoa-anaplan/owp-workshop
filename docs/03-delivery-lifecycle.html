The Delivery Lifecycle

Four phases from data load to a running, extended app

Delivering OWP Method · 2

An OWP delivery moves through four phases: a pre-project phase to confirm fit, a data-driven configuration phase, a fork into two paths depending on what the customer needs, and — where needed — extension design and delivery. The order is the same every time; how long you spend in each phase depends on the customer's data and appetite.

Phase 1 — Pre-project

The goal here is to confirm the app is a fit and set up everything the configuration phase will need.

  • Run a deep-dive demo of the app — show what it does and understand what the customer actually wants to use.
  • Hold a discovery workshop, review the functional scope, and run a data-readiness assessment: can the customer bring employee, job-role, and compensation data — yes or no?
  • Produce the Statement of Work (SOW) with high-level scope and compatibility.
📝 Templates go out early

The data templates and solution guide are shared before kickoff, during this phase. See Data Templates & Readiness for what's in them and how to use them.

Phase 2 — Configuration

This phase is where the app becomes the customer's app. It is entirely data-driven and iterative — "data is the most important part."

  • Use the templates to capture the right level of detail per dimension, the right number of hierarchy levels, and the right properties.
  • Most of the work is on the customer side — they design and extract data from their systems into the expected templates.
  • Do the settings in the config pages: currency, current date, number of roles, approvers, which workflow to deliver, and so on.

The heart of the phase is an iterative loop: load data, find what's missing or mis-coded, go back to the customer for a new file or fields, and reload.

💡 Close the loop with the customer

"You load data… and then share with the customer: Are you happy with this data? Is this your data? Do you recognize your hierarchy?"

The output of this phase is an app running with data, ready to use — followed by an onboarding workshop with the customer. Teams can duplicate the app to test multiple settings, and can restart from zero at any time using the stored templates.

Phase 3 — Two paths from a configured app

Once the app is configured and running with data, the engagement forks based on what the customer needs.

Happy as-is

The customer is satisfied with the configured app → move to test / UAT, running user-testing scenarios, then use it as-is.

Needs more

The customer needs capability beyond the app → move to extensions: an additional report, a new dataset not in OWP, or a new rule or calculation.

📝 The paths aren't strictly ordered

Test and extensions can run both ways — you might postpone testing to wait for an extension, or test first and extend afterward.

Phase 4 — Extension design & delivery

When extensions are in scope, you deliver them as a regular Anaplan change: write the stories, plan a sprint, deliver, and test.

🚨 Design not to break what works

What's different is how you design. You're changing a fully functional app of roughly 200 modules, so the focus is not to break something that's working.
