About This Workshop

The OWP Delivery Partner Workshop is a technical enablement program for Anaplan delivery practitioners — Solutions Consultants, Solution Architects, and Technical Consultants — who will implement, extend, and support the Operational Workforce Planning (OWP) application for customers.

This is not a lecture series. It is a mix of methodology, application walkthroughs, guided hands-on exploration in your own training tenant, and Q&A — designed to take you from knowing about OWP to being capable of delivering, demoing, and scoping it for real customers.

What we assume you bring

This workshop assumes you already understand what a pre-built Anaplan application is — and what it is not — and the general idea of the app-led delivery method. If your Anaplan background is in from-scratch model building, be aware that delivering a pre-built app is fundamentally different work. Loading data, configuring the app, and extending it carefully around the upgrade path are not shortcuts around custom modeling — they are the delivery method.

What we will actually do

We start with the delivery methodology: how you take a pre-built app from a signed deal, through data loading and configuration, to a running solution — and how to extend it without breaking the upgrade path. Then we tour the OWP application itself: Workforce Budgeting (WFB), the Continuous Position Plan (CPP), and the security and selective-access model. Where the workshop is hands-on, you work in your own training tenant, not on slides.

📝 OWP content reflects v2.1

The OWP content in this guide reflects the 2.1 generation of the app (one demo used a 2.0 model for a richer dataset). Screens and available features change release to release. Where you see OWP screenshots or specific behavior, confirm against the current product before using it with a customer.

How the Workshop Is Organized

The workshop has two pillars. The first teaches you how to deliver OWP; the second teaches you what the application does. The order matters — you scope and configure before you can confidently demo.

🚀 Pillar 1 — Delivering OWP

The app-led delivery method: traditional vs. app-led delivery, the four-phase lifecycle, data templates and readiness, extensions vs. core changes, the delivery timeline, and the integration/architecture picture. This is the work that happens before a customer ever sees the app.

🔄 Pillar 2 — The OWP Application

What the app produces: Workforce Budgeting (WFB), the Continuous Position Plan (CPP) — the workspace, plan actions, transfers, and skills matching — and the security and selective-access model. This is what you demo and support.

What This Workshop Intends to Accomplish

By the end, you should be able to do three things without hesitation:

1
Position OWP and run a delivery conversation — explain what OWP does, why position-level planning and the closed loop matter, and lead a discovery and data-readiness conversation. You know what data the app needs, what's mandatory, and what to ask for before kickoff.
2
Demo the application in a customer meeting — walk the CPP end to end (workspace, create/transfer positions, skills matching) and explain the WFB→CPP loop and the security model in business language. You can answer follow-up questions without going back to the Apps team.
3
Scope an engagement — look at a customer's requirements and know whether the base app handles them, whether configuration addresses them, or whether an extension is needed — and build that extension in a way that survives upgrades.

How to Think About Completing This Workshop

💡 The Right Mindset

Don't come to this workshop to watch. Come to do. The walkthroughs exist to prepare you for the guided labs and for real customer sessions — not to replace them. Log into your training tenant, follow along, and try the actions yourself. If you only read, you're only half here.

Here is how to engage with each section type:

Section TypeHow to Engage
Overview (Tell) Read actively. Flag anything unclear. Write down questions — the Q&A page and the workshop channel are where they get answered. Don't let ambiguity accumulate.
Walkthrough (Show) Open the app in your training tenant and follow along with the screenshots. Navigate to the same screen, confirm your understanding in real time. If your workspace looks different, ask in the channel.
Guided Lab (Do) Actually do the steps in your tenant. Use the quick checks and Check-my-Work to confirm you're on track. If something doesn't match, figure out why before moving on — this is where the learning happens.
Debrief questions Don't skip them. They're the bridge between doing the steps and understanding why. If you can answer them confidently, you understood the section. If you can't, you executed it.

Why the Labs and Q&A Matter

The methodology and walkthroughs tell you what to look for. The guided labs and the questions are where it becomes durable knowledge.

Labs Build Muscle Memory

Reading about a position transfer is not the same as doing one. The CPP has a specific flow — plan actions, schedule dates, backfill-vs-close decisions — and specific ways to get it wrong. You need to feel that flow, not just know it exists.

Labs Surface Real Questions

The questions that matter most — the ones that come up in customer sessions — only surface when you're doing the work. "What happens to the vacated position when a worker transfers out?" is a question you hit in the lab, not from reading about it.

Q&A Is the Field Playbook

The Q&A page collects the real questions partners raised — on data, fit, extensions, transfers, and security — with the answers given. These are the exact objections and edge cases you'll meet with customers.

Completion Is a Signal

Each guided lab has a Check-my-Work step and a debrief. If you can complete the actions and answer the debrief, you're ready. If you can't, you're not — regardless of how much of the guide you read.

Desired Outcomes

When you leave this workshop, the goal is that you can say — with evidence, not just confidence — that you're ready to support an OWP implementation. Specifically:

  • ✅ You can explain what OWP is, why it plans at position + employee level, and how it closes the plan-to-hire loop
  • ✅ You can run a data-readiness conversation — what data OWP needs, what's mandatory, and what to ask the customer for
  • ✅ You can describe the app-led delivery lifecycle and set a realistic timeline
  • ✅ You can walk the CPP end to end — workspace, create/edit positions, transfers, and skills matching
  • ✅ You can explain the WFB→CPP bridge and the security / selective-access model
  • ✅ You can scope an extension and explain the upgrade-path risk of changing the core app
  • ✅ You can position OWP as a foundation for PRP, capacity, and demand planning

Learning Objectives

By the end of this workshop, participants will be able to:

  1. Position OWP — articulate what the app does, the two halves (WFB and CPP), the closed-loop plan-to-hire problem it solves, and where it fits the broader Anaplan suite.
  2. Deliver app-led — explain the load → set → customize approach, run the four-phase lifecycle, and lead a data-readiness conversation.
  3. Handle data and templates — describe the data OWP needs, which fields are mandatory, and how to work through dirty HR data and readiness gaps.
  4. Demo the CPP end to end — walk the workspace, create and transfer positions, run skills matching, and explain the WFB→CPP bridge in business terms.
  5. Explain the security model — describe user roles, the page-security matrix, and how selective access cascades across orgs and positions.
  6. Scope extensions cleanly — distinguish configure from extend, keep changes upgrade-safe, and know when work belongs in a separate model.
  7. Look ahead — position Optimizer and OWP-as-a-foundation for PRP, capacity, and demand planning, under safe-harbor.

How WFB and CPP Connect

OWP's two halves are designed to work as one loop. Understanding the hand-off is essential context before you tour either half.

🔄 The Closed Loop

Budgeted positions are built in WFB, then published into the CPP via the bridge once the budget is closed. In the CPP they're planned, filled, transferred, and closed day to day — and every position carries an APID that keeps it linked to the HCM system through its whole lifecycle. That's the plan-to-hire loop OWP exists to close.
