A Pre-Built App, Not a Blank Model

OWP — Operational Workforce Planning — is a pre-built Anaplan app, part of the apps suite alongside IFP, Supply Chain, and T&Q. It is delivered as a working application, not built from a blank model. The version discussed in this workshop is 2.1. (A 2.0 model was used for one demo because it had a "better data set.")

📝 Version Context

Where screens or behavior are described, assume 2.1 unless noted. The 2.0 model appears in one demo only, for its data set — not because the functionality differs in a way that matters here.

Planning at Position and Employee Level

OWP is built on position management as the best-practice approach to workforce planning. You plan at both the position and the employee level:

💡 In the presenter's words

"You have employee, you have position, you plan empty position, and then you assign employees."

Data is planned "as a position, employee, location, job role, legal [entity]." You create the empty position first, then assign an employee to fill it — that ordering is the core of how the app thinks about the workforce.

The Two Halves of the Application

WFB — Workforce Budgeting

The budgeting side of OWP. This is where budgeted and planned positions are created, before they sync into the operational plan.

CPP — Continuous Position Plan

The dynamic, day-to-day operational side — a live view of the organization used by HR, Finance, and talent.

🚨 Watch the Slide Label

The slides mislabel the CPP as "continuous forecast." The presenter corrected this repeatedly: "it says continuous forecast, but it's the continuous position plan." When you present, call it the Continuous Position Plan.

The Closed Loop: Plan to Hire

The problem OWP solves is the disconnect between planning and hiring. Finance does the budget, HR plans positions, and talent recruits — and normally these run separately, with communication between them taking months.

📝 The core gap

"No link between what we're planning, what we're actually budgeting for, and what's actually getting recruited for."

OWP closes that loop by linking the plan, the budget, and the recruiting into one system. Anaplan implemented OWP internally to solve exactly this problem.

The Position Lifecycle

A position moves through the systems as a single, traceable thing:

1
Create the position in OWP — the position is planned inside Anaplan.
2
Export to HCM — the position is pushed to the HCM system (Workday in the example).
3
Filled by a worker — the position is filled in the HCM system.
4
Actuals flow back — actuals return to OWP, so the full history is visible: who filled a role over time, compensation over time, and moves.

Positions carry a state through this lifecycle:

StateMeaning
FilledA worker occupies the position.
PlannedThe position is planned but not yet open for hiring.
OpenThe position is open to be filled.
ClosedThe position is closed.

The APID — the Golden Key

💡 What holds it together

The APID (Anaplan ID) is "the thing that keeps all of this together."

The APID is a code assigned when a position is created in OWP. It is written back into the HCM system as a field, and it becomes the key that follows the position through every system for the whole lifecycle. Because the APID travels with the position, actuals and history can always be reconciled back to the position that was planned.

The Management Org

The management org (supervisory org) is central to how OWP is built:

📝 Half the application

"Half of the application is built off of this concept of management org."

Customers that have no supervisory-org concept can still use OWP, but they have to be taught it, and it has to be built with them. It is not optional structure you can skip — it underpins a large part of the app.

The Hard Fit Limit

🚨 Position-level planning is non-negotiable

If a customer refuses to plan at the position level and wants employee-only planning, "the app is very far from their needs." This is a fundamental mismatch, not something an extension can bridge. Surface it early — it is a fit question, not a scoping question.

✅ Do — Qualify the Fit

Before you go deep on OWP with a customer, confirm they are willing to plan at the position level and that they either have, or will build, a management-org concept. If either is missing, raise it now — it shapes whether OWP is the right app at all.
