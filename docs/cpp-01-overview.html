The Continuous Position Plan (CPP) is the operational half of OWP. Where WFB is the budgeting side, the CPP is the living view of the organization that people work from every day.

📝 Continuous position plan, not continuous forecast

The slides label this "continuous forecast," but that is a mislabel. It is the continuous position plan — the presenter corrected it repeatedly: "it says continuous forecast, but it's the continuous position plan."

What the CPP is

The CPP is best described in the presenter's own words — it is "this landing area really for HR and finance and talent to look at this dynamic view of the organization." It shows the organization as it changes over time: hires, transfers, promotions, sabbaticals, movement, and backfills. And it is linked to finance, so every one of those changes ties back to budget, forecast, plan, and actuals.

Who uses the CPP

The CPP is a shared landing area. Several roles come into it, each for their own view of the same org:

  • HRBPs
  • Finance
  • Management-org owners
  • Talent acquisition
  • Workforce planners
  • Comp analysts
  • Cost-center owners

The views

The CPP shows both filled positions and plan positions. Its cost and position views let you compare across the planning states, so you can see where the plan stands relative to the budget and to what has actually happened:

View What it shows
Forecast The forward-looking view of positions and cost.
Budget What was budgeted on the WFB side.
CPP The live, continuous position plan as it stands now.
Actuals What actually happened, flowing back from the HCM system.

Plan actions vs. analytics

The CPP is organized into two kinds of work. Plan actions are the things you do to the plan. Analytics are the views you look at to understand it.

Plan actions

  • Create a position
  • Create many positions
  • Edit plan positions in bulk
  • Transfer positions
  • Transfer workers
  • Worker departures

Analytics

The cost and position views that let you compare forecast, budget, CPP, and actuals across the organization, drill into a worker, a job, or an org, and understand where the plan stands.

Position-based planning

The CPP plans by position — filled, planned, or open — rather than by headcount. This is "more of a best practice for workforce planning." Planning at the position level is what makes the closed loop work: a position can be budgeted, planned, filled by a worker, and tracked all the way through, instead of being a number on a headcount line.

Directs vs. indirects: the flat management org

The management (supervisory) org list is kept flat in the front end. The reason is that people can sit at an upper level of the org and also belong to orgs below it — a strict tree does not capture that cleanly. So the flat list is rebuilt into a hierarchy in the back end, where it is used for aggregation and for security. You will see this again in Selective Access, where granting access against a flat org still cascades down the reconstructed hierarchy.
