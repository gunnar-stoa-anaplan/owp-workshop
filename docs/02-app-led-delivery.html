App-Led Delivery

Starting from a pre-built app, not a blank model

Delivering OWP Method · 1

OWP is delivered as a working application, not built from scratch. That changes the shape of the whole engagement. Before you plan a single sprint, you need to understand how app-led delivery differs from the traditional Anaplan approach you may be used to.

Two ways to deliver

Traditional Anaplan delivery

The "blank piece of paper." You build from scratch: gather requirements, run workshops, plan and review sprints, deliver each sprint, run UAT, and repeat. Everything the customer sees, you first have to build.

App-led delivery

You start with a pre-built app that already contains the structure and the logic. Your first move isn't to build — it's to bring the customer's data into a working application and then shape it to fit.

📝 The first action is data

"The first action in an Anaplan-app way for [an] application is to load data in it." You are not opening an empty model — you are feeding a real one.

The sequence: load, set, customize

App-led delivery follows a deliberate order. Each step assumes the one before it.

1
Load data — Bring the customer's data into the app so it becomes a running application with their positions, employees, and hierarchies in it.
2
Set / configure the app — Use the configuration pages to tune the app to the customer: currency, dates, roles, approvers, which workflow to deliver, and so on. Set it first to get the most out of what's already there.
3
Customize / extend only what's needed — Once the configured app is running, add or extend only the pieces the customer genuinely needs beyond what ships.
💡 Set first, then customize

The guiding rule is "set it first, and then customize" — configure the delivered app so you use the most of its capabilities before you reach for extensions.

Why start from the app

The app is not a starter template. It arrives with a large amount of working machinery already in place:

  • Hundreds of modules already built and connected.
  • Built-in best practices baked into the structure and logic.
  • Delivered workflows ready to route requests.
  • ADO connections for getting data in and out.

Rebuilding all of this from a blank model would be "months of project just to reach this amount of components." Starting from the app lets you skip straight to configuring and extending.

The anti-pattern: lift and shift

The most common mistake is trying to force the customer's current process into the app unchanged.

🚨 Don't lift and shift

Customers who try to "lift and shift" their existing process into OWP are working against the app — that "is not really what we should be doing." The value is in the best practices the app already carries.

When a customer does want to fundamentally change the core of the app, that's the moment to slow down and ask why. The right conversation is whether that thing even belongs in OWP at all, or whether it should live somewhere else. Changing the core is not the default move — it's the exception you justify.
