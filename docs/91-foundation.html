OWP as a Foundation

Feeding PRP, capacity, demand, and the agentic CFO

Looking Ahead

It's tempting to sell OWP as a point solution — the workforce-planning app. The bigger story is that OWP is a foundation. Once people, positions, skills, and performance are normalized in one place, that same data becomes the starting point for a whole set of downstream planning problems.

🚨 Safe Harbor

The downstream connections and the agentic-CFO vision on this page are forward-looking and covered by Anaplan's safe-harbor caveat. Some are in early stages or on the roadmap. Timing and scope may change.

Not a point solution

OWP is positioned as a foundation, not a standalone tool. The reason is the data it holds. When you have normalized people, positions, skills, and performance in one model, you have exactly the inputs the rest of the planning stack needs. As the team puts it: "your demand is your people."

What OWP feeds

With that normalized foundation in place, OWP can feed a range of downstream apps and use cases:

PRP — Project Resource Planning

PRP is the pointer for project, skills, and visa-type staffing questions. When someone asks "who can staff this project, with these skills, on this visa?", that routes to PRP, drawing on the OWP foundation.

Capacity planning

Normalized positions and skills feed capacity planning — matching the workforce you have and are planning against the capacity the business needs.

Demand planning

Because "your demand is your people," the same workforce data drives demand planning rather than being modeled separately.

Contact-center & GTM capacity

Contact-center planning and go-to-market capacity both sit on the same people foundation, reusing the OWP data instead of rebuilding it.

The agentic-CFO vision

Sitting on top of OWP, Finance, and PRP is an agentic-CFO / AI vision — an AI layer that reasons across the connected planning stack rather than one app at a time. OWP is the workforce piece of that foundation. This is forward-looking and was presented as a direction, not a shipped capability.

📝 Note

PRP is the natural answer whenever a partner or customer raises project, skills, or visa-type staffing. Point them to PRP and to OWP-as-foundation rather than trying to force those questions into OWP itself.

Already happening in the field

This isn't purely theoretical. A couple of customers are already linking Forecaster into OWP, connecting forecasting into the workforce foundation. And the workforce-planning-as-foundation story, along with the agentic-CFO material, was presented at Anaplan's conference, Connect.

💡 How to use this in the field

When you scope OWP with a customer, ask what they'll want to plan next — projects, capacity, demand, go-to-market. Framing OWP as the foundation for those, rather than a one-off app, changes how the customer values the data-readiness work you're asking them to do.
