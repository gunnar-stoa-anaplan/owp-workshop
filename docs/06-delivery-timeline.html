Delivery Timeline

A week-by-week plan — and why to double it

Delivering OWP Method · 5

Here is the timeline the presenter sketches for an OWP delivery — and then the honest correction he adds on top of it. Start with the optimistic version to see the shape of the work, then plan against the realistic one.

The optimistic timeline

The presenter is candid that this is the best case — "in projects, I never have seen this one." Treat it as the shape of the phases, not a schedule you can commit to.

Phase Duration What happens
Week 0 1–2 weeks Pre-project. Meet the business users, understand what data can be ingested, confirm high-level coverage, then give the green light. Templates are provided before kickoff.
Weeks 1–3 ~3 weeks Configuration. Workshops roughly every two days. Iterate the loop — take data, load data, change data, reload data — and set the pages, until the app is available.
Weeks 4–6+ 4 weeks and up Testing and extensions. Duration depends entirely on the number of extensions in scope.
📝 A best case, not a promise

The presenter has "never seen this one" run to plan. Use the table to explain the phases to a customer — not to set the delivery date.

The realistic guidance: double it

🚨 Double the time

Take the optimistic timeline and double it. Week 0 alone can take a month. Plan against the doubled version and you will be far closer to reality.

The reason is almost always the same: "the data is the long pole in the tent… the worst part." Data prep is what stretches the schedule, so start it roughly three weeks before the project formally begins.

What drives the delays

  • Customers needing their IT / Workday teams to build new reports or new fields before the data can even be extracted. That work sits outside your control and rarely moves fast.
  • Customers who don't yet know what they want — organizations with no prior workforce-planning experience take longer because the requirements are still forming as you go.
💡 Prior WFP experience speeds things up

Customers who already run a workforce-planning app move faster — for them it's largely "just connect the dots." The heavy lifting of defining the process is already done, so configuration and data prep go more smoothly.
